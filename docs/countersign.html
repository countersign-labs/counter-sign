01 · The gap

Agents can act. Nobody standardised who says yes.

Every serious agent platform agrees on one thing: before an agent spends money, posts publicly, or touches a customer, a human should sign off. But every platform invents its own private way of asking. The pause, the ping, the approval, the audit log: all locked inside one vendor's SDK.

The agent ecosystem has standardised two conversations. The third one is missing.

02 · The whole spec is four nouns

Small enough to read over coffee. Sharp enough to audit against.

Intent the ask
A small, signed JSON envelope the agent emits before any consequential action. What it wants to do, why, the risk tier, who can approve, and how long it will wait.
Route the delivery
The Intent travels to wherever the approver already lives. Telegram, Discord, Slack, email. No new dashboard to check. Adapters are pluggable and dumb on purpose.
Countersignature the receipt
The human's decision comes back as a signed, portable, tamper-evident receipt: who approved, under which policy, at what time. This is the artifact an auditor asks for.
Default the timeout rule
Every Intent declares what happens if nobody answers. Silence is never ambiguous. A routine post might default to reject in 15 minutes; a critical decision might wait a day.
